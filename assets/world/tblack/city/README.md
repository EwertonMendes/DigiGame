# Central City urban art contract

Central City's overworld composition is split into three independent layers:

1. the continuous gray micro-paver circulation field;
2. raised civic/landscape structures generated by `CentralCityUrbanPlan.gd`;
3. replaceable pixel-art street furniture loaded through `CentralCityDecor.gd`.

This separation is intentional. Buildings and planted islands define the streets through
negative space instead of painting a bright tactical-looking route on top of the gameplay
grid.

## Raised urban structure

`CentralCityUrbanPlan.gd` owns the visual foundation language:

- DigiLab, Training Center and Hospital use their already measured runtime footprints to
  derive a slightly expanded raised concrete foundation;
- foundations keep a light micro-paver top, graphite visible side faces and a restrained
  district accent;
- garden trees sit inside compact raised concrete-and-grass islands instead of directly on
  large square grass tiles;
- Central Plaza frames the digital pool with a low civic structure.

The foundations are presentation geometry only. Existing building collision/door contracts
remain the source of truth for gameplay.

## Replaceable prop catalog

The source of truth for repeatable street furniture is
`assets/resources/world/central_city_decor.json`.

Each asset entry defines:

- `path`: runtime texture path;
- `foot`: exact pixel anchor touching the ground plane;
- `scale`: authored render scale;
- `blocker`: small ground-plane walkability footprint;
- optional shader metadata.

District profiles contain stable asset IDs and fractional logical-grid positions. Replacing
a prop never requires editing section code: keep its asset ID and update the catalog path,
anchor, scale or blocker.

## Current runtime kit

The rejected SVG prototype kit was removed. Runtime street furniture now lives under
`assets/world/tblack/city/props_v2/` as transparent PNG pixel art rendered with nearest
filtering.

The intentionally small kit contains:

- benches in both isometric ground directions;
- cyan and warm civic lamps;
- long planted flower beds in both directions;
- public data terminals;
- framed holographic wayfinding signs;
- canal railings in both directions.

The palette is derived from the existing authored city exteriors: graphite structure,
light-gray civic materials, cyan data accents, warm market lighting and saturated planting.
Props are placed in deliberate groups around destinations and raised lots rather than
uniformly scattered across open pavement.

Holographic signs use `city_hologram.gdshader`. Other props are static and have no
per-frame script processing.
